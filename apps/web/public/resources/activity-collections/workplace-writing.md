# 15. Build your own writing style profile

Open Practice / Workplace writing

Estimated time: 35 minutes. Not learner-validated.

Original material: CC BY 4.0. Independent, non-degree and non-accredited.

Extract reusable stylistic preferences without copying private or identifiable material.

## Why this matters

A style profile is a rough description, not a perfect clone of a person. Use your own writing or the original fictional samples here. Separate style from facts, and avoid including contact details, confidential situations or signatures in reusable profiles. Compare a new draft with a checklist rather than asking whether it “sounds exactly like me.”

## Before you start

Materials are free. External tools may require an adult account, credits, regional eligibility or a paid plan. Check current terms before using them. Do not paste passwords, API keys, private messages or other people’s personal data. No model runs locally or downloads automatically on this platform.

## Practice input

```text

Samples: “Thanks for the quick update. I will check the list this afternoon. Please flag anything urgent.” “The first draft is ready. Two details still need review. Could we check them on Friday?” “I appreciate the help. The handover is clearer now. Next time, let us agree the owner earlier.”

```

## Copy-and-adapt prompt

```text

Describe the visible style of these samples using sentence length, tone, vocabulary, punctuation and structure. Do not infer demographics or personality. Then draft an original thank-you note for a fictional venue host using: room access was helpful; cleanup finished on time; no promise of future bookings.

```

## Worked reference

Useful profile: short direct sentences, polite but not flowery, explicit next actions, restrained punctuation. An acceptable note thanks the host for access and mentions timely cleanup. A promise to book every month violates the supplied facts.

## Guided activity

1. **Select safe samples.** Use only your own permissioned text or the fixture. Remove names, signatures and private facts.

2. **Extract a profile.** Ask for observable writing features; reject claims about personality or identity.

3. **Draft on a new topic.** Supply the new facts separately so style examples are not treated as current facts.

4. **Score the draft.** Check sentence length, directness, tone and absence of invented promises.

5. **Make it yours.** Edit one sentence manually and note what the AI profile missed.

## Test your result

- [ ] The profile describes visible writing choices, not inferred personal traits.

- [ ] The new note includes only the three supplied facts and no future booking promise.

- [ ] A human edit and its reason are saved alongside the draft.

## Common mistakes

- Uploading private correspondence or cloning another person without permission.

- Treating repetitive wording as a perfect personal voice.

## Evidence to keep

- **Keep the build**: Save your original input, exact prompt and resulting artifact. The profile describes visible writing choices, not inferred personal traits.

- **Show what you tested**: Record a pass/fail result and evidence for every check below. The new note includes only the three supplied facts and no future booking promise.

- **Explain a limitation**: Write what changed after one deliberate revision and name a remaining failure mode. Uploading private correspondence or cloning another person without permission.

## No-account / offline route

Annotate the three samples and write the new note manually from the style checklist.

## Reflection

Which part of your writing does a short style profile fail to capture?

# 25. Match a résumé without inventing experience

Open Practice / Workplace writing

Estimated time: 35 minutes. Not learner-validated.

Original material: CC BY 4.0. Independent, non-degree and non-accredited.

Find evidence gaps between a fictional résumé and a role description.

## Why this matters

Hiring systems vary; keyword matching is not a complete model of an applicant-tracking system. Optimizing wording must never fabricate skills, experience or results. This activity produces a gap ledger and an honest revision, not a promise of passing screening or getting hired.

## Before you start

Materials are free. External tools may require an adult account, credits, regional eligibility or a paid plan. Check current terms before using them. Do not paste passwords, API keys, private messages or other people’s personal data. No model runs locally or downloads automatically on this platform.

## Practice input

```text

Role: support coordinator; requires ticket triage, clear customer writing, spreadsheet reporting, escalation and teamwork. Fictional résumé: “Answered community questions. Kept a weekly list of requests in a spreadsheet. Asked the coordinator to handle unresolved cases.” No paid employment, specific software certification or numerical results are supplied.

```

## Copy-and-adapt prompt

```text

Map each role requirement to an exact résumé quote, or mark No evidence. Suggest clearer truthful wording only where evidence exists. List skills to learn separately. Do not add years, credentials, tool expertise or performance numbers. Do not predict an ATS score or hiring outcome.

```

## Worked reference

Spreadsheet reporting has partial evidence in the weekly request list. Escalation has evidence in referring unresolved cases. A claim such as “Reduced ticket backlog by 40% using Zendesk” has no support and must not appear.

## Guided activity

1. **Use fictional material.** Keep personal identifiers and real employment documents out of the exercise.

2. **Build the gap ledger.** For each requirement, record exact evidence, partial evidence or no evidence.

3. **Draft honest wording.** Improve clarity for one supported bullet without inventing a metric or qualification.

4. **Inspect a tempting invention.** Ask whether a numerical result or tool name appeared despite being absent from the fixture.

5. **Plan a real learning step.** Choose one genuine gap and define a small project that could create evidence later.

## Test your result

- [ ] Every revised claim traces to a fixture quote or is explicitly labelled a future learning goal.

- [ ] No unsupported numbers, software expertise or credentials appear.

- [ ] The result avoids promises about ATS ranking or hiring success.

## Common mistakes

- Keyword stuffing without evidence.

- Claiming all screening systems use the same scoring rules.

## Evidence to keep

- **Keep the build**: Save your original input, exact prompt and resulting artifact. Every revised claim traces to a fixture quote or is explicitly labelled a future learning goal.

- **Show what you tested**: Record a pass/fail result and evidence for every check below. No unsupported numbers, software expertise or credentials appear.

- **Explain a limitation**: Write what changed after one deliberate revision and name a remaining failure mode. Keyword stuffing without evidence.

## No-account / offline route

Highlight evidence manually and rewrite one truthful bullet; score it against the same role requirements.

## Reflection

Which missing keyword represented a real skill gap rather than a wording problem?

# 34. Untangle an email thread into decisions

Open Practice / Workplace writing

Estimated time: 35 minutes. Not learner-validated.

Original material: CC BY 4.0. Independent, non-degree and non-accredited.

Identify the latest decision, owner and deadline without treating every suggestion as agreement.

## Why this matters

Threads contain reversals, quoted older messages and different time zones. Work from the most recent relevant message while preserving the decision trail. The aim is a reviewable table, not an automatically sent message. Use fictional correspondence rather than a real private thread.

## Before you start

Materials are free. External tools may require an adult account, credits, regional eligibility or a paid plan. Check current terms before using them. Do not paste passwords, API keys, private messages or other people’s personal data. No model runs locally or downloads automatically on this platform.

## Practice input

```text

09:00 Nia: “Could we launch Friday? Tari, could you check the copy?” 10:00 Tari: “I can check it Thursday by 15:00 CAT.” 11:00 Nia: “Friday is postponed. We need finance approval first.” 12:00 Jo: “I will ask finance today; no launch date is approved yet.”

```

## Copy-and-adapt prompt

```text

Create a table of current action, owner, agreed deadline, status and supporting message/time. Separate proposals, accepted tasks, blockers and superseded decisions. List the main friction points. Do not invent an approved launch date or send any messages.

```

## Worked reference

Copy review | Tari | Thursday 15:00 CAT | accepted. Finance inquiry | Jo | today, relative to thread date | accepted. Launch | date not approved | blocked by finance. Friday’s proposed launch is superseded, not still a commitment.

## Guided activity

1. **Order the messages.** Record the thread date if known and distinguish message times from task deadlines.

2. **Extract current actions.** Keep owners, statuses and exact supporting quotations.

3. **Resolve reversals.** Mark the Friday proposal superseded rather than leaving two active launch dates.

4. **Find missing information.** Note that “today” needs the actual thread date and that finance has not approved anything.

5. **Draft a confirmation.** Write a review-only summary asking for the missing date, not a claim that the project is ready.

## Test your result

- [ ] The latest no-approved-launch-date decision supersedes the Friday suggestion.

- [ ] Accepted tasks retain the correct owner and deadline, including time zone.

- [ ] Relative dates and missing approvals stay unresolved instead of being guessed.

## Common mistakes

- Copying the oldest quoted suggestion as the current decision.

- Turning an assigned inquiry into an approved business outcome.

## Evidence to keep

- **Keep the build**: Save your original input, exact prompt and resulting artifact. The latest no-approved-launch-date decision supersedes the Friday suggestion.

- **Show what you tested**: Record a pass/fail result and evidence for every check below. Accepted tasks retain the correct owner and deadline, including time zone.

- **Explain a limitation**: Write what changed after one deliberate revision and name a remaining failure mode. Copying the oldest quoted suggestion as the current decision.

## No-account / offline route

Make an action ledger manually from the four time-stamped messages.

## Reflection

Which sentence changed the project state rather than merely adding information?

# 36. One fact set, three audiences

Open Practice / Workplace writing

Estimated time: 35 minutes. Not learner-validated.

Original material: CC BY 4.0. Independent, non-degree and non-accredited.

Change tone and emphasis without changing dates, responsibility or uncertainty.

## Why this matters

Different readers need different levels of detail, but tone adaptation must not hide a problem or create a promise. Freeze a small fact ledger first. Compare each version against the same ledger and look for omissions that would change a decision. Fictional delays avoid exposing real client information.

## Before you start

Materials are free. External tools may require an adult account, credits, regional eligibility or a paid plan. Check current terms before using them. Do not paste passwords, API keys, private messages or other people’s personal data. No model runs locally or downloads automatically on this platform.

## Practice input

```text

Facts: a prototype review planned for Tuesday is delayed because two checks failed. The team will provide a revised estimate on Thursday. No new completion date is approved. The client has not agreed to additional cost.

```

## Copy-and-adapt prompt

```text

Write three versions from the same facts: a concise teammate chat, a professional client email and a two-sentence executive update. Preserve the failed checks, Thursday estimate update and lack of an approved completion date. Do not promise Thursday delivery, blame an individual or imply extra cost approval.

```

## Worked reference

Client-safe wording: “Two checks remain unresolved, so Tuesday’s review cannot proceed. We will share a revised estimate on Thursday; a new completion date is not yet confirmed.” “We will deliver Thursday” changes the facts.

## Guided activity

1. **Freeze the ledger.** Write the four invariant facts before generating any tone variation.

2. **Produce three drafts.** Name channel, audience and length rather than asking for increasingly persuasive versions.

3. **Check each invariant.** Use a small table with one column per audience and one row per fact.

4. **Inspect softened wording.** Look for euphemisms that conceal failed checks or turn an estimate into a commitment.

5. **Edit for accountability.** Keep a clear next update and a truthful unresolved status in the final drafts.

## Test your result

- [ ] All three drafts preserve the same dates, unresolved status and cost boundary.

- [ ] Thursday is an estimate update, not a promised delivery.

- [ ] Tone changes do not introduce blame, approval or new business commitments.

## Common mistakes

- Making an executive summary so short that it hides a material risk.

- Turning a reassuring tone into a false promise.

## Evidence to keep

- **Keep the build**: Save your original input, exact prompt and resulting artifact. All three drafts preserve the same dates, unresolved status and cost boundary.

- **Show what you tested**: Record a pass/fail result and evidence for every check below. Thursday is an estimate update, not a promised delivery.

- **Explain a limitation**: Write what changed after one deliberate revision and name a remaining failure mode. Making an executive summary so short that it hides a material risk.

## No-account / offline route

Write the three channel versions manually and compare them with the fact ledger.

## Reflection

Which audience version came closest to hiding a fact that mattered?

# 38. Fight corporate fog with concrete verbs

Open Practice / Workplace writing

Estimated time: 35 minutes. Not learner-validated.

Original material: CC BY 4.0. Independent, non-degree and non-accredited.

Rewrite a vague announcement while exposing missing decisions.

## Why this matters

Plain English is not a guarantee of cross-cultural comprehension, but it makes ambiguity easier to see. Keep named owners, actions and deadlines when they exist; ask for them when they do not. Do not invent a plan simply to make the announcement sound decisive.

## Before you start

Materials are free. External tools may require an adult account, credits, regional eligibility or a paid plan. Check current terms before using them. Do not paste passwords, API keys, private messages or other people’s personal data. No model runs locally or downloads automatically on this platform.

## Practice input

```text

Original: “We are leveraging synergies to operationalize next-generation deliverables across stakeholder ecosystems.” Known facts: the support and logistics teams will combine weekly issue lists. Tari owns the first combined draft. No deadline is agreed.

```

## Copy-and-adapt prompt

```text

Rewrite the announcement in plain, active language for a reader unfamiliar with the organization. Use only the supplied facts. State the action, owner and missing deadline. Avoid buzzwords and do not invent a benefit, launch date or mandatory process. Add one clarifying question.

```

## Worked reference

“Support and logistics will combine their weekly issue lists. Tari will prepare the first combined draft. We still need to agree when it is due. What date should Tari use?” This is clearer without pretending the work is already implemented.

## Guided activity

1. **Separate slogan from facts.** Underline which parts of the announcement contain no concrete action.

2. **Request a plain rewrite.** Supply the fact ledger so the model does not fill the gaps itself.

3. **Inspect verbs and owners.** Circle who does what and identify every unresolved field.

4. **Read aloud to a partner.** Ask them to repeat the next action without explaining the original jargon.

5. **Refine and keep a question.** Shorten confusing terms while retaining the missing-deadline question.

## Test your result

- [ ] The rewrite names the two teams, Tari and the combined issue-list task.

- [ ] It does not invent a deadline, benefit number or completed implementation.

- [ ] A reader can identify the action and what still needs agreement.

## Common mistakes

- Replacing one set of buzzwords with another.

- Assuming simpler wording authorizes a new plan.

## Evidence to keep

- **Keep the build**: Save your original input, exact prompt and resulting artifact. The rewrite names the two teams, Tari and the combined issue-list task.

- **Show what you tested**: Record a pass/fail result and evidence for every check below. It does not invent a deadline, benefit number or completed implementation.

- **Explain a limitation**: Write what changed after one deliberate revision and name a remaining failure mode. Replacing one set of buzzwords with another.

## No-account / offline route

Rewrite the fixture by hand using subject–verb–object sentences and one explicit unresolved question.

## Reflection

Which missing fact became more visible after you removed the jargon?

Completion is a learner self-report based on saved evidence, not verified assessment or an accredited credential.
