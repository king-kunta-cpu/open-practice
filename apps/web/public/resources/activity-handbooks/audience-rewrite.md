# 36. One fact set, three audiences

Open Practice / Workplace writing

Estimated time: 35 minutes. Not learner-validated.

Original material: CC BY 4.0. Independent, non-degree and non-accredited.

Change tone and emphasis without changing dates, responsibility or uncertainty.

## Why this matters

Different readers need different levels of detail, but tone adaptation must not hide a problem or create a promise. Freeze a small fact ledger first. Compare each version against the same ledger and look for omissions that would change a decision. Fictional delays avoid exposing real client information.

## Practice input

```text

Facts: a prototype review planned for Tuesday is delayed because two checks failed. The team will provide a revised estimate on Thursday. No new completion date is approved. The client has not agreed to additional cost.

```

## Copy-and-adapt prompt

```text

Write three versions from the same facts: a concise teammate chat, a professional client email and a two-sentence executive update. Preserve the failed checks, Thursday estimate update and lack of an approved completion date. Do not promise Thursday delivery, blame an individual or imply extra cost approval.

```

## Worked reference

Client-safe wording: “Two checks remain unresolved, so Tuesday’s review cannot proceed. We will share a revised estimate on Thursday; a new completion date is not yet confirmed.” “We will deliver Thursday” changes the facts.

## Guided activity

1. **Freeze the ledger.** Write the four invariant facts before generating any tone variation.

2. **Produce three drafts.** Name channel, audience and length rather than asking for increasingly persuasive versions.

3. **Check each invariant.** Use a small table with one column per audience and one row per fact.

4. **Inspect softened wording.** Look for euphemisms that conceal failed checks or turn an estimate into a commitment.

5. **Edit for accountability.** Keep a clear next update and a truthful unresolved status in the final drafts.

## Test your result

- [ ] All three drafts preserve the same dates, unresolved status and cost boundary.

- [ ] Thursday is an estimate update, not a promised delivery.

- [ ] Tone changes do not introduce blame, approval or new business commitments.

## Common mistakes

- Making an executive summary so short that it hides a material risk.

- Turning a reassuring tone into a false promise.

## Evidence to keep

- **Keep the build**: Save your original input, exact prompt and resulting artifact. All three drafts preserve the same dates, unresolved status and cost boundary.

- **Show what you tested**: Record a pass/fail result and evidence for every check below. Thursday is an estimate update, not a promised delivery.

- **Explain a limitation**: Write what changed after one deliberate revision and name a remaining failure mode. Making an executive summary so short that it hides a material risk.

## No-account / offline route

Write the three channel versions manually and compare them with the fact ledger.

## Reflection

Which audience version came closest to hiding a fact that mattered?
