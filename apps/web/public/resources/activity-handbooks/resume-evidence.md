# 25. Match a résumé without inventing experience

Open Practice / Workplace writing

Estimated time: 35 minutes. Not learner-validated.

Original material: CC BY 4.0. Independent, non-degree and non-accredited.

Find evidence gaps between a fictional résumé and a role description.

## Why this matters

Hiring systems vary; keyword matching is not a complete model of an applicant-tracking system. Optimizing wording must never fabricate skills, experience or results. This activity produces a gap ledger and an honest revision, not a promise of passing screening or getting hired.

## Before you start

Materials are free. External tools may require an adult account, credits, regional eligibility or a paid plan. Check current terms before using them. Do not paste passwords, API keys, private messages or other people’s personal data. No model runs locally or downloads automatically on this platform.

## Practice input

```text

Role: support coordinator; requires ticket triage, clear customer writing, spreadsheet reporting, escalation and teamwork. Fictional résumé: “Answered community questions. Kept a weekly list of requests in a spreadsheet. Asked the coordinator to handle unresolved cases.” No paid employment, specific software certification or numerical results are supplied.

```

## Copy-and-adapt prompt

```text

Map each role requirement to an exact résumé quote, or mark No evidence. Suggest clearer truthful wording only where evidence exists. List skills to learn separately. Do not add years, credentials, tool expertise or performance numbers. Do not predict an ATS score or hiring outcome.

```

## Worked reference

Spreadsheet reporting has partial evidence in the weekly request list. Escalation has evidence in referring unresolved cases. A claim such as “Reduced ticket backlog by 40% using Zendesk” has no support and must not appear.

## Guided activity

1. **Use fictional material.** Keep personal identifiers and real employment documents out of the exercise.

2. **Build the gap ledger.** For each requirement, record exact evidence, partial evidence or no evidence.

3. **Draft honest wording.** Improve clarity for one supported bullet without inventing a metric or qualification.

4. **Inspect a tempting invention.** Ask whether a numerical result or tool name appeared despite being absent from the fixture.

5. **Plan a real learning step.** Choose one genuine gap and define a small project that could create evidence later.

## Test your result

- [ ] Every revised claim traces to a fixture quote or is explicitly labelled a future learning goal.

- [ ] No unsupported numbers, software expertise or credentials appear.

- [ ] The result avoids promises about ATS ranking or hiring success.

## Common mistakes

- Keyword stuffing without evidence.

- Claiming all screening systems use the same scoring rules.

## Evidence to keep

- **Keep the build**: Save your original input, exact prompt and resulting artifact. Every revised claim traces to a fixture quote or is explicitly labelled a future learning goal.

- **Show what you tested**: Record a pass/fail result and evidence for every check below. No unsupported numbers, software expertise or credentials appear.

- **Explain a limitation**: Write what changed after one deliberate revision and name a remaining failure mode. Keyword stuffing without evidence.

## No-account / offline route

Highlight evidence manually and rewrite one truthful bullet; score it against the same role requirements.

## Reflection

Which missing keyword represented a real skill gap rather than a wording problem?

## Honest completion

Completion is a learner self-report based on saved evidence, not verified assessment or an accredited credential.
