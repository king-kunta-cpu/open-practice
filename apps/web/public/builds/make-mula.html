ACTIVITY 1 / 35 MIN ESTIMATED

Find a problem through discovery

An offer connects a narrow customer problem to a checked deliverable and a price hypothesis. AI does not create demand. Learn through real permission-based discovery where possible, but label rehearsals and never fabricate sales, experience or testimonials. Ask three consenting potential users about their current workaround; keep simulations explicitly labelled. Inspect the supplied input before using any tool. Write the decision rules in your own words and calculate one small result independently. The reference output is an answer key for this deliberately bounded fixture, not proof of general capability. Policy for this project: Include selling, delivery, checking and support time. Payment-country eligibility must be verified before selling; no false residency, borrowed accounts or bypassing provider restrictions. Learning completion does not require earning money. For learners across regions underserved or excluded by mainstream providers: verify country eligibility, language, account/identity requirements, data terms, cost, quota and connectivity before choosing a cloud service. Use only legitimate available alternatives; never bypass restrictions or invent a country/account. No service access? Complete the labelled manual/fixture route without claiming a live AI run. Project clinic: Discovery is listening before proposing automation. Ask about the most recent time the task occurred: what triggered it, who did it, how long it took, what went wrong and what the consequence was. Avoid leading questions such as 'Would you pay for my AI tool?' Ask permission to keep anonymous notes; do not record private documents or collect payment details. Three conversations reveal hypotheses, not a representative market.

Worked example

CONSTRUCTED DISCOVERY NOTE — NOT A REAL CUSTOMER
Role: owner of a fictional repair shop.
Recent task: copied five enquiry messages into a weekly job list.
Current workaround: paper notebook; owner checks every entry.
Problem hypothesis: duplicate transcription and missing appointment dates.
Unproven: willingness to pay, available budget, accessible payout route.
NEXT QUESTION: 'Can you describe the last time a missing date caused a problem?'

  5. Keep the result and your reasoning in the lesson notes; do not mark a simulated run as live.
  6. Use the discovery guide in market-evidence.md for three permission-based conversations, or clearly label a rehearsal; separate exact observations from your interpretation.

Project clinic: Pricing is a hypothesis, not a number copied from an income guide. In the fixture, a price of 30 minus tool cost 3 and fees 2 leaves 25 before tax. Four hours include two delivering, one selling and one supporting, so the effective return is 6.25 per hour before tax. Equipment, connectivity, foreign-exchange charges and additional rework may reduce it further. A refund of 10 leaves 15 and changes the return to 3.75. If no one pays, earned income is zero.

Worked example

HYPOTHETICAL ECONOMICS — GENERIC CURRENCY UNITS
Price: 30; tools: 3; transaction fees: 2.
Net before tax: 30 - 3 - 2 = 25.
Time: delivery 2h + selling 1h + support 1h = 4h.
Effective hourly return before tax: 25 / 4 = 6.25.
Refund scenario: (25 - 10) / 4 = 3.75.
These are assumptions to test, not a quoted market price or earnings result.
