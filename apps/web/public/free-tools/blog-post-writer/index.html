FREE TOOL · WRITING

Write a blog post people finish reading

Most AI blog posts read the same because nobody briefed them properly. Here you write the brief first — reader, single goal, the three points you actually believe — then make a draft and test it against rules editors use: a promise in the headline, scannable sections, short sentences and a clear next step.

⏱ 10–20 min · Works with or without AI · No signup

You will practise

  • Turning a vague topic into one reader and one goal
  • Structuring an article with scannable H2 sections
  • Editing AI output for length, specificity and filler

    Pro moves
    • Write the headline last — once you know what the post really delivers.
    • Delete your first paragraph and see if the post gets better. It often does.
    • Read the H2s alone: they should tell the whole story.
    Questions people ask
    How long should a blog post be?
    Long enough to finish the job for one reader. For practical how-to posts, 800–1,200 words is common, but a complete 600-word answer beats a padded 2,000-word one.
    Will Google penalise AI-written posts?
    Google says it rewards helpful content regardless of how it is produced, and it acts against mass-produced pages with little value. A post with your own examples, checked facts and a clear purpose is the safe route.
    Do I need an AI key to use this?
    No. The guided template teaches the same skill with no AI at all. If you add your own free key (Groq, Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Mistral or Cerebras), the tool can also draft and revise. The key stays in your browser and requests go straight to that provider.
    Where is my work saved?
    In this browser only (local storage), until you copy or download it. Nothing you type is sent to Open Practice.
